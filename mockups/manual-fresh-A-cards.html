How I operate

What I’m good at, where I loop, and what helps. Written a piece at a time — only the parts I agreed were true.

Layer 01

My Strengths

What I’m genuinely good at, named plainly.

I think out loud to find the edges of a problem.

Talking isn’t me being unsure. It’s how I locate the shape of a thing — by the time I’ve said it three ways, I know which version is true.

Added from a conversation · April

I catch the detail everyone else walked past.

The thing that felt off in the room, the line in the email that didn’t sit right — I notice it early, often before I can say why.

Added from a conversation · April

I stay with things long after others call them good enough.

That stubbornness reads as intensity from the outside. From the inside it’s just that I can feel when a thing isn’t finished, and I’d rather sit in the discomfort than hand over something half-true.

Added from a conversation · May

Layer 02

Some of My Patterns

Loops I repeat — and what each one quietly costs.

I run on no margin.

I’ll pour the whole day into the work and leave nothing in reserve — so the people closest to me tend to meet the version that’s already past empty, not the one I’d actually choose to give them.

Added from the dishwasher conversation

When I feel cornered, I get precise instead of soft.

The sentences get shorter and more exact, and to the other person it can land as cold — even when what I’m really doing is trying not to lose the thread.

Added from a conversation · May

Layer 03

How I Process Things

The way my mind actually moves through a thing.

I need the long way round before I can take the short one.

A decision that looks impulsive from the outside has usually been turning over in the back of my mind for days — I just don’t say it out loud until the shape has settled.

Added from a conversation · May

Once something is named clearly, I move fast.

The slow part is never the doing. It’s the wait for the words to land in a way I trust.

Added from a conversation · June

Layer 04

What Helps

The conditions and small moves that make things easier.

A little margin before people changes everything.

Ten quiet minutes between the last thing and the first person, and I arrive as myself instead of as the residue of the work.

Added from the dishwasher conversation

Tell me the real thing directly.

Indirectness reads to me as a puzzle I’m failing, and I’ll spend energy decoding tone that I could have spent on the thing itself. Plain and early beats gentle and late, every time.
