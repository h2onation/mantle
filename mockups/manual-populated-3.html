ISome of My Patterns
2
I go quiet when I'm overwhelmed.
When things pile up past a certain line, you don't get louder. You disappear. The words stop coming. Your face goes neutral. To anyone watching, it looks like you've checked out or don't care. What's actually happening is the opposite. Your system is taking in too much and the only move it has is to shut the output channel.
The quiet isn't withdrawal. It's your system buying time. If someone pushes for a response during the silence, it doesn't speed you up. It adds another input to a system already past capacity.
I rehearse conversations before I have them.
Before any conversation that matters, you build the whole thing in your head. Not just what you'll say. What they'll say back. Where it could go sideways. You run it three, four, five times until you find the version that feels safe enough to actually start. By the time you open your mouth, you've already had the conversation alone. The real one never matches the rehearsal, and the gap between what you prepared and what actually happens is where the stress lives.
This isn't anxiety about talking. It's your system trying to reduce variables in an interaction you can't fully predict. The rehearsal is load-bearing. Without it, the conversation feels like freefall.
IIHow I Process Things
1
Background noise makes everything harder.
In a quiet room you can hold a conversation, read a face, follow a train of thought. Add a TV, a dishwasher, someone else talking in the next room, and the whole system starts dropping frames. You're not distracted. Your processing is being split across inputs that other people filter out automatically.
Noise isn't a preference issue. It's a processing bandwidth issue. Asking for quiet isn't being difficult. It's asking for the conditions where your system can actually run.
