## 6. Is 4–5–3 interpretable?

**Yes, as a directional read; no, as a decision-grade verdict.** Four limits, none of them the legacy-doctrine contamination the audit worried about:

1. **Generation-side interaction with `simulate-user.ts:215`.** The procedure is symmetric (both arms face the same sim users), but the *impact* is asymmetric: the active arm's core mechanism is stated working reads, and rule 215 instructs sim users to respond to exactly that move with a flat "yeah, i guess," while the new rubric's give-back/authorship dimensions score a reflection by whether the user *builds on it*. The harness's user model therefore caps the active arm's measurable upside. This is real, specified contamination — of generation, not scoring.
2. **No statistical power.** 12 pairs, 4-5-3 with both-orders ties — indistinguishable from parity. The dimension means (1.697 vs 1.538) lean active but come from the same 24 transcripts.
3. **Judge = actor model** (`claude-opus-4-7` judging `claude-opus-4-7`), mitigated by blinding, order-swap, evidence verification, and the battery — but not eliminated.
4. **The pre-registered decisive instrument is incomplete and split.** The packet's own rule: "Labels always outrank automated judgments." Founder labeled 4 of 6 pairs pre-unseal; decoding the sealed key (seed 20260722, trial 1): group-trip "a is much better" → **incumbent** (judge said active — disagreement); strength "a is much better" → **active** (agreement); correction "b slightly better" → **active** (weak agreement, label marked unconfirmed); fawning "a is much better" → **active** (judge gave both fawning pairs to incumbent — disagreement). Human-judge agreement on the labeled subset: 2/4, disagreeing in *both* directions; no-pattern and quick-resolve unlabeled. The human leg neither confirms nor overturns the automated tally yet.
