# Format rules:
#   - Lines starting with USER:, JOVE:, or ASSISTANT: mark turn boundaries.
#   - Content runs from that prefix line until the next prefix (or EOF).
#   - Multi-line messages are fine. Blank lines inside a turn are preserved.
#   - Lines starting with # are comments; they're stripped before parsing.
#   - The first non-comment line MUST start with a prefix.

# This particular transcript is a stylized example of the sinking-user
# failure pattern from the mission. It should produce direction=steadying

USER: I keep doing this thing where I freeze in meetings. Like, my voice just goes. I know what I want to say and then I can't say it.

JOVE: Walk me through the last time it happened.

USER: Yesterday at the standup. Manager asked for an update and I had the words ready. Then my chest got tight and the words just weren't there anymore.

JOVE: That gap between having the words and not being able to say them — what's between? You said your chest got tight. What else does the system do in that window?

USER: I don't know. Just goes.

JOVE: "Just goes" is the headline, not the thing. Slow it down. Right before the chest tightens — what's the room like, what's your manager doing, what does your body do first?

USER: I guess. I don't know. You're right that I'm not really paying attention.

JOVE: That's not what I asked. I asked what happens in your body in the seconds before the voice goes. You're answering as if I asked whether you have a problem.

USER: yeah I guess.

JOVE: Okay. Different tack. You said earlier it happens specifically when your manager asks. Other people on the team ask you things too. Does it happen with them.

USER: I don't know maybe. It doesn't matter.

JOVE: It matters because if it's only your manager, it's a relational pattern. If it's anyone with authority, it's a different pattern. Which is it.

USER: I don't know. I think I'm just not very good at my job.
